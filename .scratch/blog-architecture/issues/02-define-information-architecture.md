# Define the Information Architecture

Type: grilling
Status: resolved
Blocked by: 01

## Question

What are the canonical content types, top-level navigation, topic boundaries, and home-page editorial priorities for a publication that mixes articles, topic collections, and project case studies?

## Answer

The Technology Notes Channel uses five durable Topics organized around engineering problem domains rather than programming languages:

1. Systems Programming: Linux, networking, memory, and I/O.
2. Concurrency Engineering: high concurrency, performance, and reliability.
3. Realtime Systems: instant messaging, protocols, presence, and message delivery.
4. Data Systems: databases, caching, and storage.
5. Game Technology: engines, servers, and realtime synchronization.

Go, C, C++, and Python are tags because they are tools that can occur across several topics. Tags provide cross-topic discovery and must not duplicate the primary navigation hierarchy.

The canonical content types are Articles, Series, Topics, and Project Case Studies. These live within the Technology Notes, Life Notes, or Investment Notes Channel established by the later home-direction decision. Project Case Studies may describe professional work after company, client, exact scale, and sensitive operational details are removed. They preserve the engineering problem, constraints, options, trade-offs, decision, and outcome so that they demonstrate judgment rather than merely displaying a project.

The top-level navigation is Writing, Channels, Topics, Projects, and About. The publication identity links to Home. Search is an icon action rather than a navigation label. Archive is part of Writing instead of a separate top-level destination.

The home page balances an exploratory editorial path with direct retrieval through persistent navigation. Its concrete cover, Content Lobby, Channel Card, and motion structure is resolved in [Choose the Home Visual Direction](03-choose-home-visual-direction.md).

Home Card size reflects deliberate editorial emphasis, not publication date. Featured Work can therefore remain prominent while newer items remain directly discoverable.

Series and Topics remain distinct. A Series is an ordered reading path with a defined progression; a Topic is a durable, unordered body of work that continues to grow.
